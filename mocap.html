My Role

Worked on motion capture and virtual production setups that connected physical performers and live cameras with Unreal Engine in real time. My role involved configuring the tracking pipeline, establishing communication between capture software and Unreal Engine, and preparing the live camera workflow for combining real-world footage with virtual environments.

The goal was to create a reliable real-time pipeline where body movement could drive a digital character while the live camera feed could be combined with Unreal Engine's virtual environment for virtual production.

Motion Capture Pipeline

📡 OptiTrack Motive

Set up OptiTrack Motive to receive and process motion capture data from the tracking system, including body tracking and performer movement.

🔗 Motive → Unreal Engine

Configured the communication pipeline between Motive and Unreal Engine so real-time tracking data could be transmitted into the engine and used to drive digital characters.

🕺 Real-Time Body Tracking

Integrated live body-tracking data into Unreal Engine, allowing performer movement to be reflected by a digital character in real time for interactive and virtual production workflows.

🎥 Tracking & Calibration

Worked on the setup and calibration of the tracking environment to ensure the captured movement and virtual representation aligned correctly during real-time production.

Project Inspiration · Dota 2 The International AR

This project was inspired by the augmented-reality hero presentation used during Dota 2 The International, where virtual hero models were integrated into the live camera view around the physical player booths.

I wanted to reverse-engineer the underlying production workflow rather than simply reproduce the visual result. My goal was to understand how a virtual character could maintain believable spatial positioning inside a real environment as the camera moved, and how the real and virtual spaces could be aligned for lighting and shadow interaction.

Face Tracking & Audio-Reactive VFX

I also worked on a real-time face-tracking workflow using an iPhone as the facial input device. Facial movement data was captured from the device and sent into Unreal Engine, where it was used to drive a digital character's facial performance in real time.

On top of the facial tracking, I created a real-time VFX system that responded to audio input. The character's mouth area could react to the incoming audio signal, driving visual effects to create an animated mouth effect synchronized with the performance. This combined face tracking, real-time character animation, audio-driven parameters, and Unreal Engine's Niagara VFX system into a single interactive production workflow.

Live Camera & Virtual Environment

I also worked with Blackmagic Design equipment to bring a live camera feed into the production workflow. The camera was used as a moving viewpoint while the physical surroundings were combined with a digital character rendered in Unreal Engine.

To make the virtual scene match the physical location, I recreated the room and surrounding environment in Unreal Engine at 1:1 real-world scale. I measured the physical space and built the corresponding virtual geometry so that walls, floor boundaries, and other spatial elements occupied matching positions in the digital environment. This provided a spatial reference for the virtual character and helped keep the real camera view and Unreal scene aligned.

In the demonstration, I move the camera around the physical space to show that there is no physical character present in the environment. Instead, the display shows a virtual character captured and composited into the live camera view. Because the virtual environment mirrors the real room dimensions, the character can be positioned relative to the same spatial boundaries seen by the camera.

I also used a shadow catcher to capture the character's shadow and merge it onto the real ground surface. The 1:1 environment setup gives the virtual lighting and shadow system a matching spatial reference. For example, when the virtual character moves closer to a real wall, the corresponding virtual wall position in Unreal provides the correct spatial relationship for the character's shadow and lighting interaction. This helps the shadow remain visually connected to the real environment instead of behaving like a flat overlay.

This demonstrates a real-time mixed-reality / virtual production workflow: the live camera provides the real-world background and perspective, while Unreal Engine provides the virtual character, matched environment geometry, lighting, and shadow information.
